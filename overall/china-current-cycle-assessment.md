# China current economic-cycle assessment

**Information cutoff:** 2026-08-25. **Latest observation month:** July 2026 for PMI, prices, activity and credit; June 2026 for industrial profits. Release dates are shown because a strong *level* can coexist with a weakening *impulse*.

## Bottom line

**Bounded cycle label: shallow/incipient contraction, with disinflationary momentum.** This does **not** mean output is falling year on year: industrial and service output levels still grew. It means the latest broad, seasonally adjusted diffusion measures moved below 50 while hard-activity sequential growth weakened sharply and domestic-demand/credit impulses deteriorated. Inflation is positive year on year, especially upstream, but its latest impulse is downward rather than stagflationary.

- **Growth impulse: negative.** July's composite PMI, manufacturing PMI and non-manufacturing activity index all fell below 50; new orders weakened more than headline activity. Industrial production, retail sales and investment also lost sequential momentum. Exports and high-tech manufacturing remain meaningful offsets, so the contraction label is deliberately bounded rather than a claim of economy-wide recession.
- **Inflation impulse: negative/disinflationary.** Headline and core CPI remained low; CPI and PPI both fell month on month, and their year-on-year rates decelerated from June. Positive PPI is primarily a *level/base/input-cost* signal, not evidence of a current broad inflation acceleration.

## Why this is not expansion or stagflation

- **Not expansion:** positive year-on-year output is a level/growth-rate observation; July's seasonally adjusted PMI breadth, new orders, monthly industrial gain and investment all show a negative change in momentum.
- **Not stagflation:** growth momentum is weak, but CPI is only 0.5% and both CPI and PPI fell month on month. PPI's positive year-on-year level is narrowing, not accelerating.
- **Why not an unqualified contraction:** exports, high-tech output and lagged profits remain strong, and industrial/services output still increased year on year. The evidence supports a **shallow, early contraction in momentum**, not yet a broad recession claim.

## Falsification tests for the next 1–3 months

The label should be changed toward **recovery/expansion** if most of the following occur for at least two releases:

1. **Breadth:** composite PMI returns above **50**, with manufacturing and non-manufacturing new orders both rising materially (manufacturing above 50; non-manufacturing preferably above 48 rather than the July 44.4).
2. **Sequential activity:** industrial production improves to roughly **≥0.5% m/m**, retail sales strengthens clearly above July's **0.06% m/m**, and fixed investment stops contracting month on month.
3. **Domestic credit impulse:** TSF and real-economy RMB-loan flows cease undershooting the prior year; TSF-stock and RMB-loan growth stabilize or reaccelerate, with household lending turning positive rather than relying mainly on government/corporate bonds.
4. **Demand breadth:** property sales/investment declines narrow materially and manufacturing/infrastructure investment return to positive year-on-year growth.

The label should be changed toward **stagflation** if weak/sub-50 PMIs persist while core CPI accelerates above roughly **1.5% y/y** and PPI records renewed positive month-on-month increases for two months. It should be strengthened to an **unqualified contraction** if sub-50 composite/new-order PMIs persist, industrial production turns negative month on month, retail growth stalls or contracts, and credit growth continues to decelerate.

## Data limitations

- This is a real-time classification using releases available by **2026-08-25**, not later revisions. July industrial-profit data were not yet in the information set.
- Monthly year-on-year rates can be distorted by base effects; the assessment therefore gives greater weight to seasonally adjusted PMI breadth and published month-on-month changes.
- The PBOC July figures are cited through an official Shanghai municipal-government republication because the central PBOC page was not reliably indexed; the text identifies the figures as the PBOC's 2026-08-14 release.
